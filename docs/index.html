DevSweep

The safe way to clean developer caches on your Mac.

What DevSweep does

Every project you build leaves a trail — caches, build artifacts, downloaded dependencies. Over the months they quietly swallow tens of gigabytes of disk. DevSweep understands dozens of developer tools, finds the space they left behind across your home folder, and lets you reclaim it — safely.

Getting started

  1. Grant a folder. DevSweep is sandboxed for the App Store, so it only looks where you allow. Choose your Home folder to cover every cache in one step.
  2. Review and pick. Browse what it found — size and safety are shown for every row. Tick what you want gone.
  3. Move to Trash. Reclaim the space. Nothing is truly gone until you empty the Trash yourself.

How it keeps your work safe

DevSweep never deletes. Everything it clears goes to the Trash, so you can always pull it back. Each item is labelled:

Safe — rebuilds automatically; clear it freely. Review — comes back, but slowly; read before you tick. Protected — keys, credentials, settings; never touched.

A separate safety guard independently double-checks every path before anything moves. Your SSH keys, cloud credentials, and app data are off-limits by design.

Privacy

DevSweep has no network access, no telemetry, and no account. It runs entirely on your Mac and only reads the folders you explicitly grant it. Nothing about your files or usage ever leaves your computer.

Frequently asked questions

The scan isn't showing my caches. What's wrong?

Most developer caches live under your Home folder (in ~/Library and hidden folders like ~/.npm). If you only granted a specific subfolder, add your Home folder from the Folders menu (or the on-screen prompt) — that single grant surfaces everything.

Is it safe? Can I lose my work?

DevSweep only removes regenerable build output and caches, and everything goes to the Trash — never a permanent delete. Items tagged Protected (credentials, settings, the tools themselves) can never be selected. If you ever change your mind, restore from the Trash.

What does "Review" mean on an item?

It comes back like anything else, but slowly — a large re-download or a long rebuild. Each row explains what it costs, so you can decide before ticking it.

Why does DevSweep ask for folder access?

The Mac App Store requires apps to run sandboxed, which means they can only see folders you explicitly allow. Granting your Home folder lets DevSweep find your caches; you can review or remove that access any time from the Folders menu.

Does it delete files permanently?

No. DevSweep moves items to the Trash. They stay recoverable until you empty the Trash yourself.
